# Design frameworks

Use these durable sources and standards to frame evidence-backed design judgment.
No framework substitutes for product research, rendered verification, accessibility engineering, or domain-specific compliance.

## Human-centered framing

| Source | Use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [ISO 9241-210](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) | Identify users, tasks, context of use, requirements, and iterative evaluation. | The standard describes a process; do not claim certification or user fitness without relevant evidence. |
| [Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation/) | Separate discovery from definition and exploration from delivery when the problem or solution is uncertain. | Do not force every small, bounded change through all phases. |

## Understanding and control

| Source | Use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Norman: mental and conceptual models](https://jnd.org/the-concept-of-mental-models/) | Make the system’s available actions, consequences, and state comprehensible to users. | An assumed model is an inference; validate it with target users or task evidence. |
| [Norman: signifiers](https://jnd.org/signifiers-not-affordances/) | Make actions and interactive boundaries perceivable with labels, placement, shape, and feedback. | A visible cue is not proof that all users recognize it. |
| [Nielsen heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) | Review status, match to the real world, control/recovery, consistency, error prevention, recognition, efficiency, minimalist presentation, and help. | Heuristics identify review questions, not a score or compliance claim. |
| [Recognition rather than recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/) | Keep choices, state, and next actions visible or easily recoverable. | More visible information can increase cognitive load; use context and progressive disclosure. |

## Choice, movement, and perception

| Source | Use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Fitts’s law](https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/fitts-law) | Consider target size, distance, and placement for frequent or consequential actions. | It does not yield a universal target size or replace touch/assistive-technology evidence. |
| [Hick’s law](https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/hick-s-law) | Reduce or structure consequential choices and reveal complexity progressively. | Do not reduce necessary options or explanatory content solely to shorten a screen. |
| [Gestalt principles](https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/gestalt-principles) | Use proximity, similarity, common region, continuity, and figure-ground to organize related information. | Visual grouping can conflict with reading order, semantics, or user expectations. |

## Platform and accessibility guidance

| Source | Use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/) | Check platform conventions, feedback, direct manipulation, controls, and adaptation where Apple platforms are in scope. | Do not apply platform-specific convention as a cross-platform mandate. |
| [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) | Design for perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust experiences; identify questions for accessibility engineering. | Do not claim WCAG conformance from this review, a document, source inspection, or screenshots. |
